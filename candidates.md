# Candidates — Funniest Time Machine Warning Label (max 120 characters)

Judge brief (from the kit, in priority order):
1. Must read as a genuine time machine warning label.
2. Turn a real hazard of time travel (paradox, duplicate self, stranded ancestor) into the joke, not just name it.
3. Save the surprise for the last few words.
4. Keep a straight face — phrased like a genuine safety notice.
5. Waste no words; every phrase does work.

## Drafts

1. (107) WARNING: Do not meet your past self. He has seen your search history and he is disappointed in both of you.
2. (104) WARNING: If you return to find two of you, the spare must be returned to the past. Postage not included.
3. (104) CAUTION: This vehicle has no reverse. Regrets are permanent and so is your grandfather's new girlfriend.
4. (60) WARNING: Killing your grandfather voids the warranty. Yours.
5. (93) NOTICE: In the event of a paradox, please remain seated until you have finished not existing.
6. (99) WARNING: The manufacturer accepts no liability for meeting yourself. He already knows what you did.
7. (95) CAUTION: Do not alter major historical events. Minor ones are how we got the extended warranty.
8. (94) WARNING: Passengers returning pregnant by their own grandfather will be charged for two seats.
9. (83) NOTICE: This machine is not equipped with brakes, airbags, or a grandfather clause.
10. (100) WARNING: Side effects include déjà vu, déjà vu, and meeting a man who insists he is you. He is. Run.
11. (105) CAUTION: If stranded in the past, do not invent anything. We check. We are still mad about the hairdryer.
12. (102) WARNING: Do not step on butterflies. The last customer is the reason your phone charger changed shape.

## Duel scoring (vs the judge brief, criteria weighted in order)

- #4 vs #12: #12's charger twist is concrete and modern, but it names the butterfly effect rather than turning a hazard inward on the traveller. #4 turns the grandfather paradox itself into the joke and hides the sting in the final word — "Yours." — which converts a warranty clause into an existential threat. #4 wins on criteria 2, 3 and 5.
- #4 vs #10: #10's déjà vu repetition is a good gag and "He is. Run." lands late, but the label spends words on the setup. #4 is 60 characters with zero waste and a straighter safety-notice face ("voids the warranty"). #4 wins on criteria 4 and 5.
- #4 vs #5: #5's "finished not existing" is strong official calm, but the paradox is generic; #4's hazard is the canonical one and its twist is sharper. #4 wins on criteria 2 and 3.
- #4 vs #9: #9's "grandfather clause" is a clean pun, but a pun names the hazard flatly compared with #4, which makes the traveller's own existence the thing voided. #4 wins on criterion 2.
- The rest (#1, #2, #3, #6, #7, #8, #11) each spend more characters for a smaller final twist, or explain the joke (#1, #6) instead of letting the last word detonate it.

## Winner

**WARNING: Killing your grandfather voids the warranty. Yours.** (60 characters)

Why: reads instantly as a real warning label (criterion 1); the grandfather paradox is the joke's engine, not its topic (2); the entire surprise is one final word that re-reads the whole label (3); deadpan warranty language throughout (4); 60 of 120 characters, nothing wasted (5).
